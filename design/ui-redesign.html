Embr — a UI that grows

settled The window is GTK 4 + libadwaita, and this redesign is what it now implements: the rail with each device's own feature list, the pages beside it, and a Settings dialog. The toolkit comparison further down is kept as the record of that decision, not as a recommendation — spikes/004-gtk4-gui has the measurements, crates/embr-app is the result.

A proposal for the shape of the window once the app is not only a battery monitor: DPI, lighting, report rate, buttons, profiles. Plus what “follow the system theme” can honestly mean, measured on your desktop.

1 · Why today's shape stops working

  • 1One column holds every subject: battery, then DPI, then lighting, then buttons. At six subjects the card is a 2500 px scroll and “where am I” is a scroll position, not a place.
  • 2The two pages are scopes — the app, the device. A per-device feature is neither, so it has nowhere to live but inside that one card.
  • 3The rail is capped at 470 px, so a device name elides while the space beside it goes to a card that may be half empty.

2 · Proposal: device rail with its features, plus a feature page

What changed, in one line each

  1. Navigation gains a level: device → feature. The rail is the devices, the page is the subject.
  2. App settings move behind the gear and take the whole body, because they are a different scope — not a sibling of “Devices”. Rescan is what changes with a sweep, so it stays and keeps saying Rescanning….
  3. A page is cards, and a card is one subject with its rows: the existing SettingsCard / SettingRow / ChoiceRow / NumberRow become the shared vocabulary of both scopes, so a feature page is a new page, not a new dialect.
  4. New control kinds, one component each: SliderRow (DPI, brightness, pointer speed), ColorRow (a swatch and a picker), SlotList (the DPI slots), SegRow (report rate, lighting mode), and later KeyMap. Each one needs a value type in embr-core and stays dumb in the window — the app draws, core decides.
  5. Battery is a feature like the others (first in the list, and the rail still shows the level of every device), so nothing about the app's reason to exist gets demoted to a tab.

3 · The feature list comes from the device, not from the code

This is the part that makes the shell worth having: core already knows what each device answered, so it can also own the list of what the device advertises — HID++ FEATURE SET {0001}, which embr-hidpp reads today. The window draws the list it is handed and nothing else.

4 · “Follow the system theme” — what it can honestly mean

Embr is not a GTK 4 app: there is no GTK anywhere in the tree (grep -ri gtk Cargo.toml Cargo.lock crates/ finds nothing) — the window is Slint 1.18, drawing its own controls from Tokens in app.slint, with Palette.color-scheme forced to dark at line 989. So a GTK theme cannot be inherited the way a GTK app inherits one. What the desktop does publish, and what we can read instead, is this — measured on your machine a minute ago through the same portal GTK asks:

SourceValue hereWhat we do with it
portal org.freedesktop.appearance → color-scheme 1 — darkChooses the dark or light half of the tokens, and sets Slint's own Palette.color-scheme so the stock widgets (Button, Switch, SpinBox, ScrollView) follow too.
portal → accent-color #BD93F9 — your Dracula purple, the same value KWin is drawing your window border with Becomes Tokens.accent: the primary button, the switches, the selected feature, the focus rings. Today that token is a hardcoded #5aa9f7 blue that is not your desktop at all.
portal SettingChanged signalfires on a scheme/accent change A watcher thread re-reads and pushes the new palette in — the same pattern as the device thread; no restart to follow a KDE/GNOME scheme switch.
~/.config/gtk-4.0/settings.ini · gsettings gtk-theme-name=adw-gtk3-dark, gtk-font-name=Maple Mono NF 12, prefer-dark The fallback chain for desktops without a portal (scheme and font). Your call: adopting gtk-font-name would render Embr in Maple Mono NF 12 — a monospace UI. I would keep Noto Sans and say why, unless you want the mono look.
The GTK theme's own widget drawing (gtk.css, Adwaita shading, headerbars) not readable as dataOut of reach while Slint draws the controls. Matching it means either re-styling the widgets we own (a real, bounded cost) or the rewrite below.

Cost of the theming change on its own: one module in embr-app that resolves (scheme, accent) from the portal with the gsettings/settings.ini fallback, a dark and a light token set in app.slint instead of one, one Appearance row (System / Dark / Light) and one config key. It is independent of the redesign and I would land it first, so the new screens are born themed.

The other reading of your sentence: make it an actual GTK 4 app

  • What it buys: real Adwaita/GTK widgets, so every GTK theme, gtk.css and the GTK font apply by themselves; GTK's own accessibility and input; no Slint licence question.
  • What it costs: the window layer is rewritten — app.slint is 1 557 lines, and the glue that feeds it (main.rs, view.rs) roughly 1 000 — on top of the redesign itself, which would then have to be done twice or not at all for a while. GTK 4 and libadwaita are installed here (/usr/lib/libgtk-4.so.1, libadwaita-1.so), so it is a real option, not a hypothetical.
  • My recommendation: keep Slint, read the portal, and own the token palette. You get the scheme and your accent — 90 % of what “adapt the system theme” looks like in practice — for about 2 % of the work, and the redesign lands in the toolkit the device layer is already wired to. If you want Embr to be a GTK app, say so and I will scope the rewrite properly before touching anything.

5 · Choices I made that you did not specify

  • Features in the rail, not a tab strip. I put the selected device's features under its row (G HUB's left panel does the same) instead of a horizontal tab bar under the header: it keeps the second level inside the column that is already about this device. Veto it and I will move them to tabs.
  • App settings behind a gear, replacing the body, rather than staying a peer of “Devices”.
  • The narrow case turns the feature list into a chip row above the page rather than hiding it.
  • Battery stays first and the rail keeps every device's level — the app's alerting is its reason to exist, and I did not want it to become one tab among six.
  • Live apply for device settings (DPI, lighting) with a per-card Reset, and config-stored values only for the app's own behaviour (alerts, tray, notifications).
  • Profiles stay out of phase 1. G HUB's per-application profiles need a foreground-app watcher — a different feature with its own design. Phase 1 is one “default” profile per device, stored in config.toml beside the per-device alert levels that are already there.

6 · What I need from you

  1. Toolkit: Slint + follow the portal (my recommendation), or a real GTK 4 / libadwaita rewrite?
  2. Navigation: features under the device in the rail (as mocked), or a tab strip along the top of the page?
  3. Appearance control: System / Dark / Light row — and do we adopt gtk-font-name (Maple Mono NF 12) or keep Noto Sans?
  4. Scope of phase 1: the shell plus one new feature page end-to-end — I would take DPI on the G703, since it advertises ADJUSTABLE DPI {2201} and it is first on your roadmap — with the feature list wired to FEATURE SET {0001} from the start?

Nothing in the app is touched yet: this sheet is a proposal, and the mockups were drawn with the same tokens the window uses, the system accent your desktop reports, and the devices on your desk.
